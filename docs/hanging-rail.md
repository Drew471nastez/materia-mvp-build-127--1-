# Hanging rail: set it up

Creators read this guide at storelib.com/help/website-builder/hanging-rail
(lib/help/articles.ts, pictures in public/help/hanging-rail/). Keep the two
in step.

The Hanging rail section shows your clothes as they would hang in a shop: a
metal rail across the page and each garment on its hanger, side on, with a
price tag. When a visitor points at a garment it turns to face them and its
neighbours move aside. Clicking it lifts it off the rail into a focus view:
the garment flies to the left of a window with its sizes, Add to cart and the
pieces it is worn with. Closing the view is the same movement backwards: the
garment flies back onto its empty hanger while the window fades, and the page
can be scrolled and clicked at once. On a phone the row swipes and a tap opens
the same view as a sheet from the bottom.

Every template has it. In the website builder, choose Add section and look for
**Hanging rail of products**. The Streetwear drop template opens on one.

## 1. Photograph each garment on a hanger

You need two photos per garment, on its hanger: one from the side and one from
the front. A back view is optional.

- Hang the garment on a plain wall or a door, in even daylight, with nothing
  overlapping it.
- For the front view, stand square to it. For the side view, turn the hanger
  a quarter turn so the sleeve hangs down towards you. Keep the whole hanger,
  hook included, in the frame both times.
- Use the same kind of hanger for every garment if you can. The rail looks
  calmer when the hangers match.

No side view? The front view then hangs turned a little away (Turn at rest)
and turns to face the visitor the same way.

## 2. Cut out the background

The pictures must be **transparent PNG or WebP cut-outs**: the garment and its
hanger, with nothing behind them.

1. Open the photo in any background remover (most photo apps and phone gallery
   apps have one, and there are free websites that do it in one click).
2. Check the edges: white garments on white walls and the thin wire of the
   hook are the parts that go wrong most. Paint back anything it removed.
3. Crop close to the garment, with the **hook at the top centre** of the
   picture. The section hangs every picture from the middle of its top edge.
4. Crop the side view and the front view to the **same height, hook to hem**,
   so the garment does not jump when it turns.
5. Save it as PNG or WebP with transparency (not JPEG, which has no
   transparency).

Recommended sizes:

| Picture | Size | Shown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Side view | about 400 x 1000 px | on the rail |
| Front view | about 1000 x 1300 px | when pointed at, and in the focus view |
| Back view (optional) | about 1000 x 1300 px | only when a visitor presses Back |

## 3. Add the pictures and link the products

1. Add a **Hanging rail** section to your page.
2. Add one **Garment** block for each piece on the rail.
3. In each block, choose its **Product**. The title, price, sizes and product
   page come from the product, so they stay right when you change a price.
4. Upload the **Side view** and the **Front view**, and the **Back view** if
   you have one.
5. **Wear it with**: up to three products that go with this one. The focus
   view lists them under Add to cart, each with its own add button.
6. **Size on the rail**: about 85% for a tee, 100% for a hoodie, so they hang
   at their real lengths.
7. **Price tag** is optional: leave it empty to show the price, or write
   something of your own, such as "New".
8. **Note in the focus view** is one short line, such as the fabric.

Drag the blocks to change the order of the rail.

## 4. Settings worth changing

- **Colour scheme**: the rail, its words and its focus view use the scheme's
  colours. Fonts, buttons and corners are the theme's own (Theme settings).
  Background colour and Text colour can still be set apart from the scheme.
- **Garment height** and **Garment height on phones**: how big the garments
  hang.
- **Room for a garment on the rail**: how much width each garment gets. About
  30 to 40% for side views, 50% for front views turned a little.
- **Gap between garments** and **Hover side gap**: how close they hang, and
  how much room the neighbours leave a garment turned to the front.
- **Hover turn speed**: how quickly a garment turns.
- **Hanger swing**: how much hangers swing as the pointer brushes past. Set it
  to 0 for none. Visitors who ask their device for less motion never see it.
- **When the garments are wider than the screen**: let the row scroll (drag,
  swipe or arrows), or make every garment smaller so all fit.
- **Clicking a garment**: open the focus view, or go straight to the product
  page.
- **Behind the focus view**: frosted glass (the page blurred under the
  scheme's background) or the scheme's background, solid. The page never
  shows through clearly.
- **After Add to cart**: stay in the focus view (the button says it is added
  and a View cart link shows, the cart count and drawer update), or close the
  view and open the cart drawer.
- **Button style**: the theme's primary or secondary button, or an underlined
  link.
- **Background**: the scheme's colour, or a picture (a landscape works well).
  With a picture, **Soften the picture** blurs it a little, **Wash** lays the
  scheme's background over it, and **Words on frosted glass** puts the heading
  and the line under the rail on a pane of glass, so they read on any picture.
- **Under the rail**: centred name and button, or split with the name on the
  left and your line and link on the right.

## Checking it

- Point at each garment: it should turn from its side to the front with its
  hook on the rail. A garment that floats or sits low has empty space above its
  hook: crop it again.
- Open the focus view and close it again: the garment should land back on its
  own hanger.
- Open the focus view on a phone and add a size to the cart.
- If a hanger shows a grey or white halo on a dark background, the cut-out
  kept some of the wall: clean the edge in the background remover.
