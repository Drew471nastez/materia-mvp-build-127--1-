Curated galleries of web design sort the sites they keep by **style** (minimal, typographic, grid layout, unusual layout, photographic, illustrative, colourful, flat, dark, light, retro, brutalist, fullscreen, horizontal), by **type** (portfolio, e-commerce, agency, magazine, blog, personal, event, product, single page, microsite) and by **subject** (architecture, art, design, fashion, food and drink, photography, music, film, publishing, education, health, travel and hotels, furniture and interiors, non-profit, technology). The sites that get in share habits that have nothing to do with a template:

1. **One idea, held everywhere.** A studio's index set like a drawing register; a bakery's day as a shop board; a label's releases as a crate. Decide the idea in the concept and let every section serve it.
2. **Type is the image.** Large, tightly set display type in a face with character (typography-type-library), small precise labels, real hierarchy from size jumps. Many of the best sites have almost no decoration.
3. **The real thing is the hero.** The work, the product, the menu, the timetable, the room. Never an abstract gradient or a stock photo of people pointing at laptops.
4. **Rhythm changes on purpose.** Full bleed after narrow, dense after airy, one sticky or sideways moment, a list where others would put cards. Sections are different widths and densities because their content is.
5. **Grids are visible or broken on purpose.** Either a strict 12 column grid you can feel (numbers, rules, aligned captions) or a deliberately unusual layout (overlaps, offsets, a side rail). Never the default centred column for everything.
6. **Motion is restrained and specific.** A marquee for dates, a hover image reveal on an index, a slow reveal of photos, one view transition. Reduced motion respected.
7. **Navigation belongs to the design.** A header and footer made for the concept: a text index, a side rail, a huge footer wordmark, the site's own cart and search.
8. **Copy is specific.** Real names, places, times, materials and prices; headings that name the thing.

**Using the starting directions**: the brief lists directions chosen for the subject (concept.ts DIRECTIONS: style, type, palette, layout moves, motion, imagery, signature). They are seeds. Take one, change it for this creator's material, and make the signature move something a visitor will remember. Never copy a site you have seen; take the principle, not the look.
