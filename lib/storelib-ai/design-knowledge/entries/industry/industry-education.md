**Visitor**: wants a specific outcome and is judging whether this teacher and format will get them there, in the time they have.

**Course page**: the outcome as the headline -> who it is for and the format (self paced, live cohort, start date, hours per week) -> what you will be able to do, as four to six concrete abilities -> curriculum from the course's real modules, expandable -> the teacher -> student work or results if supplied -> price and what is included (lifetime access? community? feedback?) -> FAQ (level, time, refunds, access) -> enrol.

**Catalogue**: filter by level or topic only when there are enough courses (more than about eight). Each card: title, one line outcome, length, level, price. Same image ratio for all.

**Visual directions**: a textbook feel (serif, numbered chapters, margins with notes); a studio class feel (big photos of hands at work); a technical school (mono, grids, code or diagrams). Avoid the stock laptop and coffee photo.

**Motion**: an accordion for modules, maybe progress indicators. Keep it light; people read course pages carefully.

**Honesty**: never invent student counts, completion rates or "rated 4.9". Start dates and seats come from the product or event data.
