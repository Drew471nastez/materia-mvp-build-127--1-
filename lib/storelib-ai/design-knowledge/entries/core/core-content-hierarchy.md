A visitor arrives with questions in a rough order. A page is the answers, in that order, each as short as it can be.

1. What is this and is it for me? (hero: offer plus audience)
2. Why should I believe it? (real proof: work, results, press, reviews that exist)
3. How does it work? (process, curriculum, menu, what is in the pack)
4. What does it cost and what do I get? (pricing from the store)
5. What might stop me? (FAQ, policies, guarantee if one exists)
6. Do it. (one clear action, repeated at the end)

Adapt per business. A restaurant reorders: hours and location come second because that is the first doubt. A portfolio has no price; the work is the proof and the action is contact. A music producer's sample pack page puts audio previews before any text because hearing is the proof.

Rules: one primary action per page, repeated at most three times. Never two adjacent sections answering the same question. If a step has no real content (no reviews yet), skip it rather than faking it; the page is shorter and better. Alternate density: a dense section (curriculum list) after an open one (single image and line) keeps rhythm.
