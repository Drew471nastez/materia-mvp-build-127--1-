Work a brief in this order and do not skip ahead.

1. **Read what is real.** Products, prices, communities, forms, bookings and blog posts come from the store. Write down what exists before designing anything, because the page structure depends on it (a coach with a booking form gets a booking flow; a coach without one gets an enquiry form).
2. **Fill the visual concept** (see `core-visual-concept-first`). Name, mood, type, palette, layout principles, motion stance, imagery, two or three signature moves.
3. **Plan pages as jobs to be done.** For each page, write the one action it exists for and the order of evidence a visitor needs before taking it. Sections come from that order, not from a template list.
4. **Build the most important section first** (usually the hero plus the primary offer) and judge the concept on it before writing the rest. A concept that does not survive the first section will not survive ten.
5. **Check** with the anti-slop evaluator and the compiler, then look at a phone width.
6. **Repair the smallest thing that fixes the finding.** Do not regenerate a section to fix a colour.

Do: keep a short decision log (why this font, why this layout) so follow-up edits stay consistent.
Don't: retrieve every knowledge entry up front, add sections because "sites usually have them", or invent content to fill a planned section that has no real data behind it. Drop the section instead.
